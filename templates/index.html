What these metrics can and cannot tell you

Selection rate is the share of a group receiving a positive prediction. Demographic parity difference is the largest minus the smallest selection rate. Disparate impact ratio divides the smallest rate by the largest; it is undefined when all rates are zero.

TPR measures correctly identified positive outcomes, FPR measures negative outcomes wrongly flagged positive, and FNR measures missed positives. Equal opportunity difference is the TPR gap. Equalised odds difference is the larger TPR or FPR gap.

Different fairness definitions can disagree. Recorded outcomes may contain bias, and small groups can produce unstable measurements. The two-sided z-test compares only the highest and lowest selection rates. It is exploratory, without correction for selecting extrema across multiple groups. No significance test is applied to error-rate gaps.
